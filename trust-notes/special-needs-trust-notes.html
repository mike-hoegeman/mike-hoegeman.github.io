Summary of A Handbook for trustees

Purpose of a Special Needs trust (SNT)

Improve the quality of an individual’s life without disqualifying him or her from eligibility for public benefits.

Terms

  • Grantor - Person who establishes the trust and usually the funder of the trust.
  • Trustee - Person who manages trust assets and manages the trust provisions.
  • Beneficiary - Person for whose benefit the trust was established.
Typical (initial) set up is
Grantor -> parent of disabled child ( when child has no subsantial assets of their own ).
Trustee -> parent of disabled child
Beneficiary -> disabled child
  • Revocable Trust - Trust which is, by its own terms, revocable and/or amendable, meaning able to be undone, or changed.
  • Irrevocable Trust - means any trust which was established as irrevocable (that is, no one reserved the power to revoke the trust) or which has become irrevocable (for example, because of the death of the original grantor).
    • It is somewhat common for a SNT to be a Revocable trust while the Grantor/Trustee/Parent(s) is alive and then converted into an Irrevokable trust upon death of the Grantor/Trustee/Parent(s)

Special needs trust types

  • Third Party special needs trust - Trust is established by someone other than the person with disabilities (usually a parent) **** with assets that never belonged to the beneficiary ****.
    The assests must never have lived in an account of the beneficiaries even if just being transferred in/out temporarily. They also cannot be inherited in the benficiaries name. ** they must be inherited by the trust **
  • Self settled special needs trust - A trust established by the beneficiary (or by someone acting on their behalf) with the beneficiary’s funds primarily for the purpose of retaining or obtaining eligibility for public benefits. This is usually used when the assets are received as proceeds from a lawsuit.
    In general Third party trusts are used unless there is some reason the asset requirements cannot be met

Benefits for Disabled Adult Child

SS Benefits for child derived from a parent's SS benefits

  • Disabled Adult Child (DAC) - The benefit that a disabled child over 18 receives from a parent's Social Security is known as Disabled Adult Child (DAC) benefits.
    • Usually about 50% of parents SS FRA benefit and 75% of parents FRA benefit as a survivor benefit. Similar to spousal benefit.
  • Chlldhood Disability Benefits (CDB) - Another term used by Social security for Disabled Adult Child (DAC).

Medicare Benefits for child derived from a parent's SS benefits

  • Important Note: A child receiving DAC benefits is elegible for Medicare 24 months after DAC benefits begin.
  • Child is eligible for all the various Medicare supplemental plans e.g. Plan G, Plan D, etc..
    These are recommended to participate in if child is using medicare
